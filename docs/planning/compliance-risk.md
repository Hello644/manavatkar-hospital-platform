# Compliance, Privacy & Project Risk (India) — v1 Hospital Platform

## 1. DPDP Act 2023 obligations (hospital = Data Fiduciary)

**Status as of July 2026:** DPDP Act 2023 is law; the [DPDP Rules 2025 were notified 13 Nov 2025](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf) with staggered commencement — consent-manager provisions from Nov 2026, and the substantive obligations (notice, security safeguards, breach notification, erasure, children's data) from **13 May 2027**. Until then the SPDI Rules 2011 (IT Act s.43A) technically govern, but **build to DPDP standard now** — the system will be live well past May 2027. Penalties: up to ₹250 cr for failure of security safeguards, ₹200 cr for breach-notification failure — existential for a small hospital, so this is not optional polish.

**Employee biometric (face) data — the highest-risk data class in v1:**
- **Lawful basis:** DPDP s.7(i) "legitimate uses" covers employment purposes, but facial biometrics are intrusive; take **explicit written consent anyway** (signed form + digital consent record with timestamp, staff ID, consent-text version). Consent must be revocable — which forces a design requirement: **a non-biometric fallback attendance method** (PIN + photo capture, or admin manual entry) for refusers/revokers. This doubles as the dispute-resolution mechanism (see risks).
- **Purpose limitation:** notice must state face data is used *only* for attendance. Do NOT reuse the camera feed for surveillance/CCTV analytics. Store **face embeddings (numeric vectors), not gallery photos**, where feasible; embeddings are still personal data but reduce breach impact.
- **Retention/deletion:** delete face template + enrollment photos within 30 days of employee exit (automate: HR "deactivate staff" action triggers template purge; keep only the attendance *records* — date/time punches — which are ordinary employment records, retain 3 years for labour-law disputes).
- **Storage:** biometric templates encrypted at rest (AES-256), on the local server only — never in the cloud backup unencrypted; ideally exclude raw templates from cloud backup entirely and re-enroll after disaster.

**Patient health data:**
- **Notice + consent at registration:** plain-language notice (English + local language — Rules require availability in scheduled languages) itemizing data collected (demographics, diagnosis, Rx) and purposes (treatment, records, legally required retention). One-time consent at first registration, stored with version + timestamp; a printed bilingual A4 notice at the registration desk plus a checkbox in the registration screen is proportionate for this size.
- **Children (<18):** verifiable parental/guardian consent required; DPDP Rules exempt clinical establishments from some children's-data restrictions to the extent needed for health services, but the registration form must capture **guardian name + relationship for minor patients** — put this field in v1.
- **Data principal rights:** patients can demand access/correction/erasure — but **erasure yields to legal retention duties** (NMC record-keeping, below). Practical v1 features: export a patient's record as PDF; correction workflow with audit trail; refusal-of-erasure letter template citing retention law.
- **Security safeguards (Rule 6):** encryption at rest and in transit (TLS even on LAN), role-based access control, access logging, backups, and **retention of security logs ≥1 year** (Rule requires 1 year of logs/traffic data). Designate a named contact person for data queries (the owner/admin is fine — not a Significant Data Fiduciary, so no DPO/audit/DPIA obligations, and a 10–50 bed hospital will almost certainly not be notified as an SDF).
- **Breach duty:** notify affected individuals "without delay" and the Data Protection Board (intimation promptly, detailed report within 72 hours). Practical prep: a one-page breach-response SOP + a pre-drafted notification template stored with the admin.

## 2. Medical record retention (drives DB design: never hard-delete)

- **Erstwhile MCI Code (Indian Medical Council (Professional Conduct, Etiquette and Ethics) Regulations 2002, Reg. 1.3.1):** indoor/inpatient records **3 years** from commencement of treatment; still the operative baseline because the [NMC RMP (Professional Conduct) Regulations 2023](https://www.nmc.org.in/rules-regulations/national-medical-commission-registered-medical-practitioner-professional-conduct-regulations-2023-reg/) were notified Aug 2023 then **held in abeyance** — but they signal the direction: **3 years from date of last contact, including outpatient records, and digital-format maintenance**.
- **Common state/practice expectations:** Clinical Establishments Act states and state nursing-home acts typically expect 3–5 years; central-government hospital convention is OPD 5 years / IPD 10 years; **medico-legal cases (MLC): 10 years to indefinitely**; pediatric records: until patient turns 21 (age of majority + limitation). Consumer Protection Act limitation is 2 years but courts condone delay, so litigation exposure realistically runs 5–10 years.
- **Design consequence:** prescription and visit data must be **immutable and soft-delete only, retained indefinitely by default** (storage cost for text records of a 50-bed hospital is trivial — a few GB/decade). Add an `is_mlc` flag on visits. Corrections create new versions; originals preserved. Retention policy document: "clinical records ≥10 years, never auto-purged; biometric templates purged on exit; security logs 1–3 years."

## 3. Legally valid prescription (defines the Rx module's required fields)

- **Who prescribes:** only a Registered Medical Practitioner (RMP) registered with a State Medical Council/NMC (or dentists with DCI, AYUSH practitioners with their councils — for allopathic drugs, MBBS+ RMPs only). **Nurses/pharmacists cannot prescribe** — the system must restrict Rx creation to users with role=doctor and a stored registration number.
- **Mandatory prescription elements** (MCI 2002 code + Drugs & Cosmetics Rules 1945, Rule 65): doctor's full name, qualifications, **State Medical Council registration number** (NMC 2023 additionally mandates displaying the unique EMRB registration ID); clinic/hospital name & address; date; patient name, age, sex (address required for Schedule H/H1/X drugs); drug name — **generic name required by NMC norms** (print generic prominently, brand optionally in brackets — legibly, ideally capitals); strength, dosage form, dose, frequency, duration, total quantity; **doctor's signature**.
- **Schedule-drug rules:** Schedule H — supply only against signed Rx. **Schedule H1** (higher-risk antibiotics, some psychotropics): pharmacist keeps a separate register 3 years; Rx must carry patient address. **Schedule X + NDPS drugs**: duplicate prescription, copy retained 2 years — flag these drugs in the formulary; the module should print "duplicate copy" and warn the doctor. Store the schedule class (H/H1/X/OTC/NDPS) as a field on every formulary drug.
- **Signature validity:** a printed prescription with a **wet-ink signature is unquestionably valid — recommend this for v1** (print → doctor signs → hand to patient). A scanned/typed signature image is NOT a valid signature under the IT Act 2000; legally valid *digital* signing requires an IT Act-recognized electronic signature (**Aadhaar eSign or a DSC token**). Options if digital sharing is wanted: (a) print + wet sign only — zero cost, zero risk (recommended v1); (b) Aadhaar eSign per Rx via an ESP (NSDL/eMudhra, ~₹1–3/signature, needs internet — conflicts with offline requirement); (c) DSC USB token per doctor (~₹1,500/2 yr, works offline but clunky in a browser). Defer (b)/(c) to v2.
- **Telemedicine Practice Guidelines 2020** (Appendix 5 to MCI 2002 code): apply only if consulting/sending Rx remotely. Rx may be shared as a signed/digitally-signed photo/PDF via email/WhatsApp; registration number must be shown; **"prohibited list" (Schedule X, NDPS) cannot be prescribed via telemedicine**; certain drugs only after video consult. For v1 (in-person OPD, printed Rx) this is out of scope, but if the "share Rx PDF to patient's WhatsApp" feature is added, gate it on these rules.

## 4. Audit logging for medico-legal defensibility

Append-only, tamper-evident (hash-chained rows or WORM table; no UPDATE/DELETE grants), each entry: `timestamp (NTP-synced, ISO 8601 +05:30), actor_user_id, actor_role, action, entity_type, entity_id, before/after snapshot (JSON), source_ip/device`. Events that MUST be logged:
1. Prescription: create, edit (new version), **print and every reprint** (reprint count printed on the Rx as "Duplicate"), void/cancel with reason.
2. Patient record: create, demographic edits, merges, **every view of a patient's clinical history** (who looked at whose record — key for privacy defensibility).
3. Attendance: every punch (with match confidence score + captured frame thumbnail retained 30–90 days for disputes), **manual overrides/corrections by admin with reason**, enrollment/re-enrollment/template deletion.
4. Auth & admin: login/logout, failed logins, password resets, user create/disable, **role/permission changes**, consent records created/withdrawn.
5. System: backup success/failure, restore events, data exports, config changes, system clock anomalies.
Retention: security/auth logs ≥1 year (DPDP Rules), clinical-touching logs 3+ years (align with record retention). Provide an admin-only, read-only log viewer with filters — courts/councils ask "who changed this and when."

## 5. ABDM/ABHA

ABDM (Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission) is India's national health-data exchange: patients get a 14-digit **ABHA number** + ABHA address (`name@abdm`), facilities register in the **Health Facility Registry (HFR)**, doctors in the **Healthcare Professionals Registry (HPR)**, and certified software (as a Health Information Provider, milestones M1/M2/M3) can create ABHAs, link visit records as FHIR documents, and share them with patient consent via the consent-manager gateway. **Recommendation: do NOT integrate in v1.** Integration needs internet at point of care (conflicts with the offline-first requirement), an ABDM sandbox certification cycle (realistically 4–12 weeks of a small team's effort), and yields little immediate value to a small private OPD. But do these in v1 so retrofit is cheap: (1) `abha_number` (14-digit) and `abha_address` nullable fields on the patient table; (2) UUID primary keys, ISO 8601 timestamps; (3) doctor table stores HPR ID (nullable) alongside SMC registration number; hospital settings store HFR ID (nullable); (4) store prescriptions as **structured line items** (drug, strength, form, frequency, duration — not a free-text blob) so they can later be emitted as ABDM's FHIR `MedicationRequest`/Prescription profile ([NRCeS FHIR profiles](https://www.nrces.in/ndhm/fhir/r4/index.html)); (5) register the facility on HFR now via the web portal (free, no software needed — good faith signal and prerequisite anyway). Snomed/LOINC coding: skip in v1, field-level structure is enough.

## 6. Top 10 project risks & mitigations

| # | Risk | Mitigation |
|---|------|-----------|
| 1 | **Face-recognition errors cause staff disputes** (false absents, twins/veils/lighting, salary deductions contested) | Store match-confidence + captured frame per punch (30–90 day retention); PIN+photo fallback punch; admin correction workflow with mandatory reason + audit log; policy: FR is *evidence*, admin decision is authoritative; pick threshold favoring false-reject over false-accept; 2-week parallel run with the existing register before payroll depends on it |
| 2 | **Doctor adoption resistance** (paper Rx takes 30 s) | Target <60 s per Rx: per-doctor favorite-drug lists, Rx templates for top 20 diagnoses, copy-last-visit, keyboard-first UI; pilot with the owner-doctor first; keep paper as sanctioned fallback for month 1; measure time-per-Rx and iterate before rollout |
| 3 | **Single-server hardware failure** = hospital-wide outage | Server-grade box with RAID-1 SSDs + UPS; a cheap cold-standby mini-PC with a nightly restore-tested image; documented 30-minute manual failover; paper fallback SOP printed at reception |
| 4 | **Data loss** (disk death, ransomware, accidental delete) | 3-2-1: local RAID + nightly encrypted snapshot to an on-prem USB/NAS + encrypted cloud backup (restic/borgbase or S3) when online; **monthly restore drill**; soft-delete only; backup failure alerts to owner's phone |
| 5 | **Solo/small dev team (bus factor 1)** | Boring stack (e.g., Django/Postgres), everything in git with README + runbook, infrastructure-as-script, admin passwords in a sealed envelope with owner, fixed-scope v1 contract with handover docs as a deliverable, avoid exotic dependencies |
| 6 | **Scope creep** (pharmacy, billing, IPD, lab "while you're at it") | Signed one-page v1 scope (the 3 modules); change requests go to a written v2 backlog; time-boxed v1 (e.g., 4 months); demo every 2 weeks so the owner sees progress without adding features mid-build |
| 7 | **Power/network outages** | UPS on server + tablet + switch (≥30 min); tablet queues punches locally and syncs when LAN returns; app is LAN-only dependent (no internet in the critical path — verify with an "unplug the router" test); printed token/Rx fallback pads |
| 8 | **Staff privacy pushback on biometrics** (refusals, union/informal complaints, attrition) | Consent-first enrollment with bilingual notice; visible alternative (PIN+photo) so consent is genuinely free; templates-not-photos messaging; deletion-on-exit policy shared in writing; owner announces purpose (attendance only, no CCTV analytics) at a staff meeting |
| 9 | **Data breach / ransomware on the LAN** (Wi-Fi shared with patients, penalties up to ₹250 cr) | Separate staff/patient VLANs or at minimum a patient guest Wi-Fi isolated from the server; server firewall allows only 443 from LAN; OS auto-updates; disk encryption; offline backup copy (ransomware-proof); breach SOP + notification template pre-drafted |
| 10 | **Spoofing/buddy-punching of the attendance tablet** (photo of a colleague) | Liveness detection (blink/motion — e.g., Luxand/FaceIO-class SDK or on-device ML Kit + anti-spoof model), tablet physically wall-mounted with camera angle unsuited to holding up phones, random stored frames audited weekly by admin, confidence threshold tuning |

(Adjacent: regulatory drift — DPDP Rules fully bite May 2027 and NMC 2023 regs may exit abeyance; assign the owner a yearly compliance-review task.)

## 7. Pre-build inputs checklist (owner must supply)

1. **Letterhead assets:** hospital logo (SVG/PNG ≥600 px), full legal name, address, phone, email, registration/license numbers to print on Rx (Clinical Establishment/state nursing-home reg. no. if applicable) — plus a scan of the current printed letterhead for layout matching.
2. **Doctor roster:** for each RMP — full name, qualifications (as to be printed), State Medical Council name + registration number (+ NMC/EMRB unique ID if issued), specialty, signature block preference, OPD days/hours.
3. **Staff list:** name, employee ID, role (nurse/ward boy/reception/lab/admin…), department, shift pattern (shift names with start/end times, weekly-off rules, rotation cycle), grace-period policy for "late" (e.g., 10 min), half-day/leave rules — needed to define the late/absent dashboard logic.
4. **Current prescription samples:** 10–20 real (anonymized) paper prescriptions from each doctor — defines drug-name habits, layout, and the target print format.
5. **Drug formulary:** top-200 drug list (or pharmacy purchase register export) with brand name, generic, strength, form; will be seeded with schedule classification (H/H1/X). Decision: generic-first printing policy sign-off.
6. **Consent & notice language:** approved bilingual (English + state language) text for (a) staff biometric consent form, (b) patient privacy notice at registration — drafts to be provided by developer, owner/lawyer signs off.
7. **Hardware inventory:** printer make/models at each OPD desk (A4 vs A5 Rx size decision), whether thermal printer wanted for tokens, existing server/PC/tablet hardware, Wi-Fi router/switch details, UPS availability, internet provider & typical uptime.
8. **Operational parameters:** OPD timings, consultation fee structure (printed on token/receipt?), token numbering scheme preference, expected daily OPD volume, departments list.
9. **Policies to ratify (one page each):** record-retention policy, biometric data deletion-on-exit policy, breach-response contact chain, who gets admin access.
10. **Accounts:** cloud storage account for backups (or approval to create one), domain/email for alerts, ABDM HFR registration credentials (register facility now even without integration).
